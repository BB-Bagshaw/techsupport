Ticket Health: Open Backlog & Time-to-Close

A distribution-based review of how long Tech Support tickets stay open, and how long they take to close, measured against the stated 7-day auto-close policy.

Prepared by Matt Bagshaw — Operational Analytics Manager

The Question We're Testing

Concerns raised that the team may not be handling tickets correctly, specifically around closing them out or following up appropriately. We were told there is a policy: tickets should auto-close at 7 days if there's been no customer interaction (phone, email, or chat).

This analysis tests that claim directly using ticket-level duration data, distinguishing between tickets still open today and tickets that have already closed.

If the 7-day policy is functioning as described, we should see a sharp drop-off in ticket volume right around day 7, both in what's currently open and in how long tickets take to close.

Sample Size: 180 days (cases created Apr 2 – Sep 29, 2026) | 111K Cases

Scope & Data Quality Checks

Before trusting the numbers, we validated the population being measured:

  • IsClosed vs. Status — confirmed 100% agreement between the boolean flag and the categorical status field; no sync issues found.
  • Case object scope — discovered other internal teams use the Salesforce Case object purely for their own queue management, unrelated to customer support. These were excluded.
  • Window truncation — early 30/90-day pulls artificially capped ticket age; extended to a 180-day window to capture the true tail.
  • Full raw extract — 110,926 valid cases (10,155 open / 100,771 closed); 0 duplicate Case IDs; 16 malformed export rows removed. Case duration reconciles exactly to Closed − Created.

What the Shape Tells Us

If the auto-close policy were active, this histogram should show a cliff at day 7 and almost nothing surviving beyond it.

Instead, after an initial intake bump, volume settles into a flat plateau of roughly 500–730 tickets per two-week interval, sustained from around day 14 through day 180.

Putting It Together

The 7-day rule is not missing — it is partially automated and inconsistently applied:

  • A meaningful share of tickets (roughly a third) do resolve quickly, consistent with genuine first-contact resolution.
  • A timed close process exists, but only on the Waiting for Customer path, and it misses a large share of eligible tickets.
  • Tickets in Pending Closure or Assigned have no working mechanism pulling them toward closure, and that's where the open-ticket plateau accumulates.
This points toward a partially broken closure mechanism, rather than a simple team performance or compliance issue.

What the Open Backlog Actually Is

10,155 open tickets    55% waiting on a customer and past a system-defined close deadline    only 19% waiting on an agent
Past deadline = Waiting for Customer past 7 business days, or Pending Closure past 2 business days. Agent-owned = Assigned, Waiting for You, ReOpened, Needs Callback, New, Working. Internal = Waiting for Bluebeam/Partner, Research, Escalated, Jira Update.

Most of the Backlog Isn't Agent Work

  • 5,544 tickets (55%) are waiting on a customer and already past the system's own close deadline, with a median age of 99 days. Fixing the automation could clear them without any agent time.
  • 3,175 of the 4,161 tickets over 90 days old are in this past-deadline group.
  • 1,952 tickets (19%) are agent-owned. Tier 1 CS holds 1,719 of them, and three owners hold 1,588 — volumes that suggest queue-holding accounts rather than individual workloads. To validate.
  • 2,502 tickets (25%) are waiting on a customer and still within their deadline — the process working as designed.
The capacity question (next phase) should be tested against the ~2,000 agent-owned tickets, not the full 10,155. Using the total would overstate the agent workload by roughly five times.

Most Teams Miss the Target — But the Gap Isn't Uniform

  • 13 of 15 sub-teams have a median open-ticket age above the 7-day target, confirming this is a systemic gap, not one team's performance issue.
  • Tier 3 APAC (124.6d), Tier 2 US Bravo (107.5d), and Tier 3 US (105.9d) carry the oldest backlogs.
  • Tier 2 DACH is the exception: median age of 5.8 days and none of its open tickets past a close deadline — proof the target is achievable under current tooling. Tier 2 Nordic (7.3d) and Tier 2 UK (7.6d) sit right at the line.
Tall, inconsistent boxes (e.g. Tier 2 US Alpha, Tier 2 APAC) paired with very young outlier tickets suggest a bimodal pattern: new intake is handled reasonably, but a portion of the backlog stalls indefinitely and is never cleared.

Case Data Is One Input, Not the Whole Picture

  • This view reflects ticket duration only. It does not yet account for scheduled hours, time on phones, queue assignment complexity, or PTO/coverage gaps.
  • Partnering with Business Process Management to layer in workforce and scheduling data before drawing final conclusions on individual performance.
  • Goal: build a framework that separates capacity/staffing problems from individual execution problems, so the right fix gets applied to the right root cause.
Case-level data establishes where the problem lives. Workforce data (next phase) will establish why.

New Layer: Does Duration Affect Satisfaction?

Beyond speed, we tested whether ticket age correlates with customer satisfaction (CSAT), using survey responses tied to Case ID on closed tickets.

Data note (system latency): CSAT-to-case match rate is 91.5% (2,371 of 2,590 survey responses). The ~8% gap is attributable to system latency between independently-refreshing case and survey extracts — both use a rolling 180-day window, but on different refresh schedules, so records near the boundary can fall inside one snapshot and outside the other at the moment of extraction. This is an expected characteristic of the current extraction method, not a linkage failure. Final analysis will set a a distinct date range to align cases with survey results.

CSAT Declines Sharply the Longer a Ticket Takes to Close

2,371 closed, matched cases    Org-wide CSAT (top-2-box) 76.2%    CSAT measured as score of 4-5 on a 1-5 scale
Top-2-box CSAT (% scoring 4 or 5) on "overall support received," bucketed by time-to-close. Closed tickets only.

Satisfaction Holds Steady, Then Collapses Past ~7 Days

  • CSAT is stable and healthy through day 5 (81.8%–82.7%), then declines meaningfully starting at the 7-day policy threshold (74.2%).
  • By 14–30 days, CSAT has nearly halved from its early-window level (52.8%), and continues falling to 41.2% for tickets open 60+ days.
  • This is a threshold effect, not a gradual decline: the data supports the idea that the 7-day target isn't arbitrary — it appears to mark a real inflection point in customer patience.
