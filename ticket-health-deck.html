Ticket Health: Open Backlog & Time-to-Close

A distribution-based review of how long Tech Support tickets stay open, and how long they take to close, measured against the stated 7-day auto-close policy.

Prepared by Matt Bagshaw · Working draft for Director & Data Engineering review · Not yet presented to COO

The Question We're Testing

The COO has raised concern that the team may not be handling tickets correctly, specifically around closing them out or following up appropriately. We were told there is a policy: tickets should auto-close at 7 days if there's been no customer interaction (phone, email, or chat).

This analysis tests that claim directly using ticket-level duration data, distinguishing between tickets still open today and tickets that have already closed.

If the 7-day policy is functioning as described, we should see a sharp drop-off in ticket volume right around day 7, both in what's currently open and in how long tickets take to close.

Scope & Data Quality Checks

Before trusting the numbers, we validated the population being measured:

  • IsClosed vs. Status — confirmed 1:1 agreement between the boolean flag and the categorical status field; no sync issues found.
  • Case object scope — discovered other internal teams use the Salesforce Case object purely for their own queue management, unrelated to customer support. These were excluded.
  • Window truncation — early 30/90-day pulls artificially capped ticket age; extended to a 180-day window to capture the true tail.

Net effect: population narrowed to Tech Support–created cases only, removing roughly 74% of raw open-case volume that belonged to unrelated teams.

Open Tickets: Aging, Not Draining

n = 11,985 open tickets  |  Median age 66 days  |  P90 150.9 days  |  84.1% exceed the 7-day policy

What the Shape Tells Us

If the auto-close policy were active, this histogram should show a cliff at day 7 and almost nothing surviving beyond it.

Instead, after an initial intake bump, volume settles into a flat plateau of roughly 300–450 tickets per week, sustained from around day 14 through day 150+.

Putting It Together

The 7-day rule does not appear to function as an automated, enforced cutoff:

  • A meaningful share of tickets (roughly a third) do resolve quickly, consistent with genuine first-contact resolution.
  • Once a ticket survives the first couple of days without resolving, there is no consistent mechanism pulling it toward closure.
  • The open-ticket plateau is effectively the accumulation of tickets that missed that early window and then had nothing forcing them forward.
This points toward a missing or non-functioning closure mechanism, rather than a simple team performance or compliance issue.

Next Steps

  • Segment both distributions by team, agent, and status to see whether stalling is concentrated or systemic.
  • Validate the 48-hour fast-close proxy against the true first-touch resolution custom field.
  • Investigate the secondary peak around day 11–13 in the closed-ticket data, likely tied to a specific workflow step.
  • Confirm with process owners whether a 7-day auto-close mechanism exists in the system today, or whether it's a stated policy with no technical enforcement.
