Ticket Health: Open Backlog & Time-to-Close

A descriptive review of open-case inventory, observed closure durations, and satisfaction patterns, with closure-process hypotheses for validation.

Prepared by Matt Bagshaw — Operational Analytics Manager

Purpose and Scope

I reviewed the Nexus case cohort to describe open-case aging, observed closure times, status/deadline patterns, and customer satisfaction.

The reported seven-day inactivity rule is a validation question—not a universal seven-day case-age limit.

Creation-date cohort: Apr 2–Sep 29, 2026 • 110,926 cases • approximately six months

What the Age Distribution Shows

  • A substantial portion of analyzed cases remains open at advanced ages.
  • Many two-week age bins contain roughly 500–730 cases; the oldest bins fall outside that range.
  • The snapshot identifies aged inventory, not net accumulation over time or cases that will never close.
  • Closure-policy compliance must be tested against qualifying status/inactivity deadlines and exceptions—not total case age.

Fast Closures and Longer Durations

  • 36,962 closed cases (36.7%) closed within 48 hours. This is a fast-close measure, not confirmed first-contact resolution.
  • 54.0% of closed cases took more than seven calendar days. Longer time can include customer waiting, investigation, and administrative delay.
  • Reported peaks around days 6–7 and 11–12 are consistent with timed administrative closure, but event history is needed to confirm attribution.

Closure-Process Hypotheses

  • Fast closures coexist with substantial aged open inventory.
  • System-account and status-timing patterns suggest automation-related activity concentrated around Waiting for Customer.
  • Aged Pending Closure inventory warrants review of exit rules and eligibility.
  • These findings identify apparent process gaps; they do not establish a broken mechanism or individual noncompliance.
Notes: combine descriptive duration, status, flags, and last-modifier associations. Confirm active flows/jobs, customer-interaction resets, status history, and exceptions with Business Systems before assigning root cause.

System-Account Associations

  • 41,369 closed cases (41.1%) were last modified by one account identified in the original analysis as a system account.
  • 99.8% of that group had reported evidence of a Waiting for Customer path.
  • 74% had a warning flag, compared with 10% in the comparison group.
  • Timing patterns are consistent with automation but do not identify the closure actor conclusively.

Inventory Is Not Labor Demand

  • 5,544 cases exceeded calculated customer-waiting/closure thresholds; their reported median age was 99 days.
  • 3,175 of 4,161 cases above ninety days (76.3%) were in that group.
  • 1,952 cases were in agent-action statuses; Tier 1 CS held 1,719, with 1,588 across three owners. Validate whether those owners are queue-holding accounts.
  • Automation remediation may reduce administrative inventory after eligibility review; it is not necessarily effort-free.
  • Capacity analysis must include arrivals, closures, follow-up, investigations, escalations, and communications—not only a status snapshot.

2,371 of 2,590 responses matched (91.5%); 219 did not (8.5%). Boundary/refresh differences are a possible explanation—not a proven explanation for all unmatched records.

Rates remain provisional pending fixed-window alignment, unmatched-record review, and response-versus-unique-case validation.
